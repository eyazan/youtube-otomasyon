# Challenger premium v1: narration script (English)

Offline prototype. Not approved for publishing.

- **Voice used in the video:** Edge `en-US-AndrewNeural`, the same free voice the published Challenger Short used.
- **Read:** six beats, with designed pauses between them.
- **Measured length:** 50.9 s of narration, which makes a 51.7 s film.
- **Words:** 134.

## Opening hooks considered

| | Hook | For | Against |
|---|---|---|---|
| **H1 (chosen)** | "Less than a second after liftoff, Challenger was already leaking." | It opens on the cause, not the outcome, and raises the question the film answers: how could it fly 73 s with a leak? An authentic NASA photo of that smoke fills frame 0. It is not the title read aloud. | The smoke is small on a phone, so it needs a marker. That marker is the ring drawn at 0.5 s. |
| H2 | "Seventy-three seconds after launch, Challenger broke apart." | Instantly recognisable. | It is both the published title and the published opening (A), so it repeats what the viewer just read. It also leads with the moment the crew was lost, which pulls the opening toward the breakup footage the original looped. |
| H3 | "The night before launch, engineers said: not below fifty-three degrees. Challenger launched at thirty-six." | Strong human stakes. | Two numbers in the first 3 s. It can imply that every decision-maker heard the warning; the Commission found that Levels I and II did not. It also spends the payoff before the mechanism is understood. |

**Why H1.** It is the only option that meets all four tests:
- it is new to someone who has just read the title;
- the film's own causal chain answers it;
- it is backed by a primary photograph that can fill frame 0;
- it does not open on the crew's death.

H3's material is kept for the payoff. By then the mechanism is clear, and the Commission's nuance fits: the objection never reached the top officials.

## Final script with evidence

E1–E3 are the Rogers Commission Report, Vol. 1, Ch. III, IV and V (nasa.gov/history/rogersrep). E4 and E5 are NASA's own photo captions.

| Line | Narration | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| L1 | Less than a second after liftoff, Challenger was already leaking. | E1: first smoke at 0.678 s at the right SRB aft field joint. E2, Finding 15: that smoke was the first sign of the seal failure. |
| L2 | Smoke was puffing from a joint on the right booster, sealed by two rubber O-rings. | E1: puffs of increasingly blacker smoke, 0.836–2.500 s. E2: a primary and a secondary O-ring. |
| L3 | That morning was fifteen degrees colder than any launch before. | E1: 36 °F, 15 degrees colder than any previous launch. |
| L4 | And cold rubber springs back slowly. | E2, Finding 9: resiliency is directly related to temperature. |
| L5 | So when ignition flexed the joint open, the O-rings couldn't keep up, and hot gas blew past them. | E2: the gap opening begins on ignition, and the O-rings may not follow the gap. Finding 1: the leak began at or shortly after ignition. |
| L6 | Then the smoke stopped, and Challenger flew on. | E1: last smoke above the joint at 2.733 s. |
| L7 | Fifty-eight seconds in, a flame broke through and swept onto the external fuel tank. | E1: flame at 58.788 s; plume deflected onto the External Tank from 60.238 s. |
| L8 | At seventy-three seconds, the tank failed, and Challenger broke apart. | E1: External Tank failure at 73.124 s; breakup began at about 73 s. |
| L9 | All seven crew members were lost. | E1. |
| L10 | The night before, engineers had recommended not launching below fifty-three degrees. | E3: "the engineering people would not recommend a launch below 53 degrees Fahrenheit". |
| L11 | Their managers reversed that advice, and NASA's top launch officials never heard it. | E3: Thiokol management reversed "at the urging of Marshall". The objections "never reached" the Level I/II officials. |
| L12 | The seal failed in the cold. But the warning failed the night before. | Editorial summary of E2 (failure of the pressure seal) and E3 ("a serious flaw in the decision making process"). It is not a quote. |

## Removed during fact-checking

- **"Burned residue briefly plugged the gap."** Chapter IV does not state it, so the line was replaced by L6, which only describes what the cameras recorded.
- **"Officials who gave the final go."** It became "NASA's top launch officials", which is the Commission's own Level I/II wording.

## Claims in the published script (A) that the premium script does not repeat

- **"They were overruled."** Too simple. The engineers' own managers reversed the recommendation, and the people at the top never heard it.
- **"Today, that warning is taught to every engineer."** No source is given for it.
- **"Seven astronauts were killed, in front of the world."** True, but it was the opening framing of the loss. The premium film states it once, plainly (L9).
